# Reality Engineering Website Publication Map

## Objective

Create a public home for Reality Engineering that allows a casual visitor to understand the category in minutes and a serious reader to examine the complete canon, evidence, objections, applications, theology, and development history.

The site should feel like an interconnected body of thought rather than a folder of PDFs.

## Primary route

`/reality-engineering`

Purpose: define the category, explain why it matters, show the core model, and route readers into the corpus.

Primary message:

> **We help people and organizations move from the reality they have to the reality they are trying to create—by changing the systems that shape what is possible.**

Secondary message:

> **Human agency is the center. AI is the amplifier.**

Governing question:

> **Can we call what we created good?**

## Information hierarchy

### Layer 1 — Understand

For readers with two to five minutes:

- landing page;
- simple definition;
- current reality → engineered bridge → desired reality diagram;
- why now;
- AI’s role;
- governing question;
- three application examples.

### Layer 2 — Believe or challenge

For readers deciding whether the concept is serious:

- Foundations;
- Constitution;
- Goodness Constraint;
- Hope;
- Conscious Reality;
- Limits;
- FAQ.

### Layer 3 — Practice

For builders, operators, creators, and organizations:

- Method Specification;
- method artifacts;
- case template;
- Goodness Review;
- AI-use review;
- application guides;
- casebook.

### Layer 4 — Inspect

For researchers, critics, journalists, partners, and legal reviewers:

- source register;
- claims ledger;
- legacy-draft audit;
- case maturity;
- provenance timeline;
- version history;
- open questions.

### Layer 5 — Theological origin

For readers interested in the spiritual foundation:

- The Echo of God;
- theological references;
- relationship to the public discipline;
- explicit doctrinal guardrails.

The theological path should be visible and respected without being required for entry into the public method.

## Landing-page flow

1. **Hero** — category and promise.
2. **The problem** — people and organizations live inside inherited or accidental systems.
3. **The definition** — full and short forms.
4. **The model** — current reality → bridge → desired reality.
5. **Why now** — AI compresses intention-to-consequence while increasing cost and responsibility.
6. **What it is not** — manifestation, AI filmmaking alone, automation alone, control, or a promise of perfect outcomes.
7. **The method** — Explore → Engage → Evolve.
8. **The Goodness Constraint** — can we call what we created good?
9. **Applications** — personal, organizational, experiential/media, economic, community.
10. **Case evidence** — visible maturity labels and claim boundaries.
11. **Reading paths** — public, practitioner, research, theological.
12. **Corpus access** — all papers, downloadable archive, version.
13. **Nexark relationship** — practitioner, not owner of all human transformation.
14. **Closing invitation** — begin with the reality you are living in.

## Canon page template

Every canonical page should render:

- title and subtitle;
- canonical ID;
- version and date;
- status badge;
- one-sentence purpose;
- table of contents;
- main body;
- related canonical documents;
- supporting Federalist Papers;
- evidence references;
- open questions;
- “suggest a correction” path;
- previous / next navigation;
- permalink to each heading.

## Federalist Paper template

Every essay should show:

- paper ID;
- constitutional and canonical references;
- evidence IDs;
- case IDs;
- claim-status callouts where necessary;
- strongest objection;
- open questions;
- related readings;
- publication and revision history.

Federalist Papers may be more personal in voice than canonical papers. They should still maintain claim boundaries.

## Evidence Center

The Evidence Center should contain five tabs or routes:

1. **Sources** — E-ID, source class, supported claim, caveat, review date.
2. **Claims** — canonical claim, classification, support, status.
3. **Cases** — maturity level, artifacts, measurements, missing evidence.
4. **Corrections** — material corrections and why they were made.
5. **Open research** — hypotheses not yet established.

Do not use the number of citations as a visual proxy for truth. Surface relevance, source quality, caveat, and claim scope.

## Cross-reference system

Every concept should have a stable ID.

Examples:

- `RE-C01` — Constitution;
- `RE-C05` — Hope;
- `RE-F07` — AI Filmmaking essay;
- `E-006` — IEA energy source;
- `RE-CASE-001` — promotional-film case;
- `RE-G-CR` — Conscious Reality glossary entry, if granular glossary IDs are later added.

References should resolve into contextual previews where possible:

> **Hope** — the disciplined willingness to orient toward a future good that is not guaranteed… [Read RE-C05]

This creates Bible-like movement across the corpus without making repetition appear to be independent evidence.

## Visual system recommendations

The visual system should communicate rigor and possibility.

Recommended elements:

- generous white or neutral space;
- strong editorial typography;
- one accent color for the desired/new reality;
- a restrained neutral for the current reality;
- the paired-star/compass visual as a state-transition symbol where brand appropriate;
- diagrams with clear labels rather than decorative abstraction;
- evidence-status badges that are legible but not alarming;
- rounded content containers where consistent with Nexark’s design system;
- accessible contrast and font sizing;
- no dark-on-dark sections that reduce readability.

The spiritual companion may have a more contemplative visual treatment, but it should remain inside the same publication system.

## Trust and disclosure UI

Each page should make the following visible:

- **Founder belief**
- **Theological interpretation**
- **Observation**
- **Hypothesis**
- **Research-supported claim**
- **Case evidence**
- **Illustration**

Use brief labels with hover or expandable definitions.

AI-assisted authorship disclosure should live in the provenance and methodology pages rather than interrupting every paragraph.

## Accessibility and comprehension

- Write the primary text for a thoughtful high-school reader.
- Define specialized language on first use.
- Provide text alternatives for diagrams.
- Avoid making motion necessary for comprehension.
- Preserve keyboard navigation.
- Use descriptive link text.
- Provide a print-friendly view.
- Make evidence tables responsive and readable on mobile.
- Allow theological and secular reading paths without treating either reader as an outsider.

## Metrics that match the ethic

Measure:

- reading completion by document;
- movement among related concepts;
- casebook engagement;
- source-register use;
- correction submissions;
- downloads of the corpus;
- practitioner use of method templates;
- and qualitative feedback about clarity and application.

Do not optimize solely for time on page, compulsive scrolling, or repeated visits without value.

The site should help people understand and act, not engineer attention for its own sake.

# The Reality Engineering Evidence Standard

## 1. Why an Evidence Standard Is Necessary

Reality Engineering is being developed in an environment where artificial intelligence can produce polished arguments, plausible statistics, invented citations, confident summaries, and language that sounds more settled than the underlying idea.

That creates a particular danger for a new discipline.

If every persuasive sentence is treated as truth, the corpus may become internally consistent while remaining externally fragile. A reader may encounter one unverifiable statistic and begin to doubt the ideas that did not require that statistic in the first place.

The Evidence Standard protects the work from that failure.

Its purpose is not to remove belief, philosophy, theology, interpretation, or founder conviction.

Its purpose is to name each kind of statement honestly.

> **A claim should never appear more proven than it is.**

---

## 2. The Six Claim Classes

Every material claim in the canon or public essays belongs to one of six classes.

## 2.1 Foundational belief — B

A normative commitment the discipline chooses to hold.

Examples:

- Technology should expand human possibility without making human responsibility meaningless.
- A desired state must be judged by more than efficiency.
- Human dignity should not be traded away for convenience.

A foundational belief may be supported by philosophy, theology, law, or public ethical frameworks, but it is not “proven” like an empirical measurement.

**Required treatment:** state it as a commitment, explain its implications, and do not disguise it as a scientific finding.

## 2.2 Founder observation — O

A pattern observed through lived or professional experience.

Examples:

- Making an idea concrete through a working prototype can give a founder clarity they did not have while the idea remained abstract.
- AI-generated first drafts can make a document sound complete before its claims have been examined.

**Required treatment:** identify the context, avoid universal language, and convert the observation into a hypothesis when making a broader claim.

## 2.3 Hypothesis — H

A proposition that should be tested.

Examples:

- A documented Human Boundary will improve trust and adoption in AI-assisted workflows.
- Organizations that measure burden transfer will make different automation decisions than organizations that measure only hours saved.

**Required treatment:** state what evidence would support, weaken, or falsify it.

## 2.4 Case evidence — C

Documented evidence from a specific intervention in a specific context.

Examples:

- A prototype changed a particular leadership decision.
- A workflow reduced a measured handoff delay in one team over a defined period.

**Required treatment:** include R₀, R★, intervention, contact period, evidence, limitations, and the exact claim the case supports.

Case evidence does not automatically support a universal conclusion.

## 2.5 Research-supported claim — R

A factual claim supported by traceable external evidence.

Examples:

- An official report documented a particular adoption rate in a defined survey.
- A peer-reviewed paper proposed a particular definition of hope.

**Required treatment:** cite the primary source when reasonably available, preserve its population and date, and avoid extending the finding beyond what the source supports.

## 2.6 Open question — Q

A question the discipline has not resolved.

Examples:

- How should a Reality Delta combine qualitative experience with quantitative metrics?
- Which forms of AI assistance increase long-term capability rather than dependency?

**Required treatment:** keep the uncertainty visible and invite research rather than filling the gap with confident language.

---

## 3. Evidence Hierarchy

Sources should be chosen according to the claim.

A practical hierarchy is:

1. **Primary evidence** — original research paper, official dataset, standard, law, technical documentation, direct project record, interview, or system log.
2. **Authoritative synthesis** — major research report or systematic review that clearly documents methods and sources.
3. **High-quality secondary interpretation** — reputable analysis that accurately represents primary work.
4. **Industry evidence** — vendor reports, surveys, benchmarks, and case studies with visible methods and incentives.
5. **Anecdote** — useful for generating questions or illustrating experience, not for establishing prevalence or causality.
6. **AI-generated assertion** — no evidentiary value until independently traced and verified.

The highest source is not always necessary for every sentence. The more consequential, surprising, precise, or contestable the claim, the stronger the source should be.

## 4. Fact-Verification Protocol

Before a factual claim enters the public canon:

### Step 1 — Isolate the claim

Write the claim without rhetoric.

Example:

> “Eighty percent of resolutions fail by February.”

### Step 2 — Identify the variables

- Which population?
- Which year?
- What counts as failure?
- What was measured?
- By whom?
- Over what period?

### Step 3 — Find the original source

Do not accept a chain of blogs repeating one another as verification.

### Step 4 — Read the relevant method or source context

Confirm that the number means what the prose claims it means.

### Step 5 — Check date and stability

A current adoption rate, law, model capability, price, or public opinion result can become stale quickly.

### Step 6 — Check for dispute, retraction, or methodological concern

A famous finding may be contested even when accurately cited.

### Step 7 — Decide whether the number is necessary

If the argument remains strong without the statistic, remove the number rather than carry weak evidence.

### Step 8 — Enter the claim in the Claims Ledger

Record source, scope, wording, status, verification date, and any caveat.

### Step 9 — Write to the evidence

Do not make the source support a stronger claim than it actually does.

### Step 10 — Preserve a path for correction

Version the document and update the claim when better evidence appears.

## 5. Rules for Numbers

Numbers create an impression of certainty and should be handled carefully.

### 5.1 No orphan statistics

Every nontrivial number needs a traceable source and context.

### 5.2 No precision beyond the evidence

Do not report exact percentages when the underlying method supports only a rough estimate.

### 5.3 Preserve the denominator

“Most users” means little without knowing which users, how selected, and how many.

### 5.4 Distinguish forecast from observation

A projection is not an event that has already happened.

### 5.5 Distinguish survey intent from behavior

What organizations expect to do is not the same as what they did.

### 5.6 Distinguish correlation from cause

A relationship between two variables does not establish that one produced the other.

### 5.7 Do not stack uncertain numbers into certainty

Several projections from different methods do not become a single established future merely because they point in the same direction.

### 5.8 Prefer an honest sentence over a weak statistic

“Many people struggle to sustain behavior change” may be more defensible than a dramatic number attached to an unreliable source.

## 6. Rules for Case Studies

A case study is not a testimonial expanded into a narrative.

It must identify:

- the context;
- the baseline;
- the intervention;
- the contact period;
- the evidence collected;
- the outcome;
- the uncertainty;
- and the claim boundary.

### 6.1 The counterfactual question

Ask what likely would have happened without the intervention.

This may not be knowable with confidence in a single case. The uncertainty should be stated.

### 6.2 Burden transfer

Record not only what work disappeared but what work appeared elsewhere.

### 6.3 Founder effect

Record whether the outcome depended on unusual founder involvement, relationships, expertise, or effort.

### 6.4 Durability

Distinguish a short-term output from a capability that persists.

### 6.5 Negative evidence

Include resistance, failure, low adoption, maintenance cost, and unintended consequence.

### 6.6 Claim boundary

State explicitly:

- what the case supports;
- what it suggests;
- and what it does not prove.

See [The Case Study Standard](../evidence/case_study_template.md).

## 7. Rules for Theological and Philosophical Claims

Theological language should be presented as theology, interpretation, witness, or hypothesis—not empirical science.

For example:

> “AI is the echo of the echo”

is a spiritual and symbolic interpretation of the relationship among God, humanity, and human-made intelligence. It is not a measurable technical property of a model.

The corpus should distinguish:

- scriptural reference;
- theological tradition;
- founder interpretation;
- symbolic analogy;
- and public ethical translation.

This distinction does not weaken spiritual meaning. It protects it from being used as pseudo-scientific proof.

## 8. Rules for AI-Assisted Writing

AI may support:

- organization;
- synthesis of provided material;
- objection generation;
- clarity editing;
- source discovery;
- comparison;
- and consistency checks.

AI must not be treated as the source of:

- a founder belief the founder has not approved;
- a statistic without verification;
- a quote without locating the original;
- a personal story that did not happen;
- a client outcome that is not documented;
- or a citation that has not been opened and checked.

Every document should pass four reviews:

### Founder truth review

Does this reflect what Sam actually believes and would say?

### Evidence review

Are factual claims sourced to the required standard?

### Voice review

Does the writing sound authored, specific, and human rather than manufactured from generic rhetorical patterns?

### Contradiction review

Does the document conflict with the Constitution, definitions, evidence, or another canonical paper?

## 9. Citation Architecture

The website corpus uses two layers of citation.

### Internal cross-reference

Canonical concepts link to their defining documents and sections.

Example:

> See [The Goodness Constraint](04_goodness_and_stewardship.md).

### Evidence ID

External factual claims use evidence IDs that link to the [Evidence Register](../evidence/source_register.md).

Example:

> AI adoption reached a specified level in a defined survey [E-003].

This keeps the public prose readable while preserving a direct path to the source, method, date, and caveat.

---

## 10. Confidence Labels

When useful, claims may be labeled:

- **Established for this corpus** — definition or constitutional commitment approved as canonical.
- **Strong external support** — multiple high-quality sources or a strong authoritative synthesis.
- **Supported** — one or more credible sources appropriate to the claim.
- **Preliminary** — early or limited evidence.
- **Contested** — meaningful disagreement or methodological uncertainty exists.
- **Unverified** — not suitable for public factual use.
- **Retired** — previously used but removed because it was wrong, misleading, stale, or unnecessary.

The label describes evidence status, not emotional confidence.

---

## 11. Correction Protocol

When an error is discovered:

1. confirm the error;
2. assess which documents depend on it;
3. correct the public document;
4. record the change and date;
5. update the Claims Ledger;
6. retain the prior version where appropriate for provenance;
7. state the correction publicly when the error materially affected the argument;
8. examine why the review process failed;
9. improve the process rather than treating correction as embarrassment.

A discipline that cannot correct itself is not practicing Evolve.

---

## 12. Publication Gate

A document is ready for canonical public release when:

- the founder has approved the beliefs and voice;
- every factual claim is traceable;
- the strongest version of a reasonable objection has been considered;
- limitations are visible;
- internal terms are used consistently;
- links and evidence IDs resolve;
- no client or personal confidentiality is violated;
- the document contributes something not already better stated elsewhere;
- and the work remains aligned with the Constitution.

The standard is not perfection.

It is defensibility, honesty, and a visible path for correction.

> **The purpose of evidence is not to make Reality Engineering sound certain. It is to make the discipline trustworthy enough to learn in public.**

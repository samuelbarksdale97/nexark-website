# The Constitution of Reality Engineering

## Preamble

Human beings have always changed reality.

We build shelter against weather. We develop language to carry memory. We create laws, markets, schools, companies, tools, rituals, art, and technology. We shape environments, and those environments shape us in return.

What has changed is the rate.

Artificial intelligence and connected technology have compressed the distance between intention and consequence. A person can now research, generate, simulate, communicate, automate, and build with capabilities that once required institutions. A small team can alter the information, choices, workflows, and experiences of thousands of people. A system can spread before its creators fully understand what it will do in contact with the world.

This creates extraordinary possibility. It also creates responsibility.

Reality Engineering exists to help people and organizations move deliberately from the reality they are living in toward a reality worth creating. It treats transformation as a practice of observation, design, action, feedback, and stewardship. It places human agency at the center and technology in service to it.

This Constitution defines the commitments that govern the discipline. Methods may improve. Tools will change. Applications will expand. These principles should remain.

---

## Article I — Reality Before Narrative

We begin with what is true.

A Reality Engineer does not start by defending a preferred story. They start by examining the reality people are actually experiencing.

That means naming constraints without dramatizing them, possibilities without exaggerating them, and outcomes without taking credit for what has not been proven.

We distinguish:

- what we believe;
- what we observed;
- what we infer;
- what we hope;
- what we have tested;
- and what reliable evidence currently supports.

An attractive narrative cannot substitute for an accurate diagnosis. A statistic cannot enter the canon merely because it strengthens the argument. Uncertainty is not weakness. It is part of telling the truth.

**Commitment:** We will not engineer belief by hiding reality.

See [The Evidence Standard](11_evidence_standard.md).

---

## Article II — Human Agency Is the Center

Reality Engineering exists to expand meaningful human agency.

Agency is the practical ability to perceive options, make informed choices, act, learn from consequences, and participate in shaping what happens next.

Technology may advise, generate, automate, predict, and coordinate. It must not quietly become the unquestionable author of human decisions. A person should remain able to understand what a system is doing at the level required by the stakes, challenge it, refuse it where appropriate, and locate who remains responsible.

> **Human agency is the center. AI is the amplifier.**

Agency does not mean pretending that everyone begins with equal options. It means building more honest and usable options within real constraints while also working to change unjust constraints.

**Commitment:** We will use technology to increase human capability, not to make human participation meaningless.

---

## Article III — The Goodness Constraint

The ability to create a reality does not make that reality worthy of creation.

Every intervention must answer more than “Will it work?”

It must also ask:

- What does this make easier?
- What does it make harder?
- Who benefits?
- Who carries the cost?
- Whose voice is missing?
- What behavior does it reward?
- What happens if it scales?
- Does it preserve truth, dignity, consent, and responsibility?
- Can we call what we created good?

“Good” is not reduced to profit, efficiency, engagement, growth, or the satisfaction of the person commissioning the system. A reality may be desirable to one person and harmful to everyone around them. Personal definition of success does not create permission to exploit, deceive, dominate, or externalize harm.

**Commitment:** We will place an explicit standard of good above capability.

See [The Goodness Constraint](04_goodness_and_stewardship.md).

---

## Article IV — Technology Should Expand the Human Experience

Technology should never replace the human experience merely because replacement is possible. It should expand what is possible within human life.

The work must preserve room for judgment, relationship, culture, care, creativity, rest, responsibility, faith, intuition, embodiment, and meaning. These are not inefficiencies waiting to be removed. They are part of what makes an outcome worth pursuing.

Automation should remove avoidable burden before it removes meaningful participation. AI should help a person think, build, learn, or act more capably before it is used to make the person invisible.

**Commitment:** We will not mistake the removal of people for the improvement of a system.

---

## Article V — Diagnose Before You Design

Every person and organization already lives inside a reality formed by interacting systems.

Before changing that reality, we must understand:

- what is happening;
- who experiences it;
- what created it;
- which parts are intentional;
- which parts are inherited or accidental;
- what should remain;
- what is causing the visible burden;
- and what the desired change would mean in practice.

A solution built before diagnosis often automates the wrong behavior, scales an unresolved contradiction, or treats a symptom as the system.

**Commitment:** We will explore reality before attempting to engineer it.

See [The Method Specification](03_method_specification.md#explore).

---

## Article VI — Build With People, Not Around Them

People affected by a system should be involved at a level appropriate to the stakes.

Involvement does not require every decision to be made by committee. It requires that lived experience, resistance, edge cases, cultural knowledge, and human consequences are treated as design information rather than noise.

A system imposed around people without their understanding or participation may produce compliance while destroying trust. A technically correct intervention can still fail because it misunderstands the reality it enters.

**Commitment:** We will treat participation as part of the engineering work, not as a courtesy after the design is finished.

---

## Article VII — Reality Engineering Is a Contact Sport

A plan is not proof. A prototype is not transformation. A generated interface is not a living system.

A system becomes real when it enters contact with people, behavior, culture, constraints, edge cases, incentives, consequences, and time.

Contact reveals what diagrams hide. It shows whether people understand the system, whether they trust it, whether it transfers burden rather than removing it, whether it changes behavior, and whether the desired state was defined correctly in the first place.

**Commitment:** We will put the smallest useful intervention into responsible contact with reality and learn from what happens.

---

## Article VIII — Feedback Is a Form of Humility

No first design deserves automatic permanence.

Reality Engineering assumes that every intervention is incomplete. We therefore build feedback into the system, listen to the people inside it, compare intended and unintended outcomes, and revise what no longer serves.

Documentation is part of feedback. It allows learning to outlive memory, success to be examined rather than mythologized, and failure to become useful rather than hidden.

**Commitment:** We will allow evidence to change our minds and our systems.

See [The Evidence Standard](11_evidence_standard.md) and [Case Study Standard](../evidence/case_study_template.md).

---

## Article IX — Constraints Are Real

Reality Engineering is not a claim that an individual can think their way beyond every material condition.

Bodies, money, time, law, geography, disability, discrimination, trauma, institutional power, technical limitations, ecological limits, and chance all shape what is possible. Some constraints can be navigated. Some can be reduced. Some require collective action. Some must be accepted for a season. Some should be resisted.

Engineering begins by taking constraints seriously.

Agency exists within limits. Acknowledging limits does not require surrendering all agency. Affirming agency does not permit us to deny the limits.

**Commitment:** We will hold structural reality and personal responsibility together without using either to erase the other.

---

## Article X — Success Is Defined Personally and Judged Relationally

Every person defines success differently.

For one person, success may mean freedom, family, health, ownership, service, mastery, peace, creative expression, community, wealth, or time. For an organization, it may mean resilience, growth, trust, quality, usefulness, or the ability to serve people better.

Reality Engineering does not impose one destination on every life.

But success is not purely private. Every desired state exists inside relationships and shared systems. A person cannot declare an outcome good simply because it satisfies them. The effects on employees, customers, family, community, society, and environment also matter.

**Commitment:** We will honor plural definitions of success while refusing definitions that require the diminishment of others.

See [Conscious Reality and the Meaning of Success](07_conscious_reality_and_success.md).

---

## Article XI — Capability Creates Responsibility

The more power a person or organization has to alter reality, the greater their duty to consider consequence.

AI can multiply speed, reach, persuasion, automation, surveillance, and production. That multiplication applies to wisdom and care, but it also applies to bias, deception, extraction, and error.

We will not treat the cost of AI as somebody else’s problem. Compute has physical infrastructure. Automation has labor effects. Generated media has truth effects. Data use has privacy effects. Delegated reasoning has dependency effects. Every benefit has a context and every scale has an externality.

**Commitment:** We will account for the material, human, informational, social, and environmental costs of the realities we engineer.

See [AI as Amplifier](08_ai_amplifier_cost_responsibility.md).

---

## Article XII — Access Should Expand, Not Power Merely Concentrate

AI and digital systems can place capabilities once reserved for large institutions into the hands of individuals and small organizations. That possibility should be cultivated intentionally.

Abundance does not mean infinite resources or costless production. It means more people having meaningful access to knowledge, tools, time, creative capacity, coordination, and the ability to build.

Democratized access is not achieved by giving people tools without literacy, protection, or ownership. Nor is it achieved when the value created by many flows only to those who control the infrastructure.

**Commitment:** We will work toward systems that distribute usable capability, preserve human ownership where possible, and allow more people to participate in creating value.

---

## Article XIII — Hope Must Become Practice

Hope is not confidence that every plan will succeed.

Hope is the disciplined willingness to orient toward a future good that is not guaranteed, imagine and build pathways toward it, and continue acting while reality is still incomplete.

Hope without truth becomes fantasy. Hope without agency becomes waiting. Hope without pathways becomes frustration. Hope without action becomes performance. Hope without feedback becomes stubbornness. Hope without community becomes unnecessarily fragile.

**Commitment:** We will treat hope as an operating posture that generates responsible action, not as a promise that reality owes us a particular result.

See [On Hope](05_hope.md).

---

## Article XIV — The Discipline Must Remain Open to Correction

Reality Engineering is a developing discipline. It should not become a closed ideology that protects its founder, vocabulary, or institutions from scrutiny.

Definitions should be stable enough to use and open enough to improve. Methods should be tested. Claims should be sourced. Case studies should include failure and unintended consequence. Practitioners should be able to disagree without being treated as disloyal.

The purpose of canon is not to end inquiry. It is to create a common foundation from which inquiry can become more precise.

**Commitment:** We will version our doctrine, document material changes, and allow reality to correct Reality Engineering.

---

## The Constitutional Test

Before a Reality Engineering intervention is approved, ask:

1. **Truth:** Are we accurately describing the current reality and our uncertainty?
2. **Agency:** Does this increase meaningful human capacity and choice?
3. **Goodness:** Is the desired state worthy of pursuit?
4. **Participation:** Have the people affected been meaningfully considered or involved?
5. **Constraint:** Are we respecting material, human, institutional, and ecological limits?
6. **Contact:** What is the smallest useful intervention we can test responsibly?
7. **Feedback:** How will we learn what the intervention actually changes?
8. **Cost:** Who pays, who benefits, and what externalities are created?
9. **Responsibility:** Is a human or accountable institution still answerable for the outcome?
10. **Stewardship:** If this succeeds and scales, will we still be able to call it good?

A system that cannot answer these questions is not ready to shape someone else’s reality.

---

## Closing Covenant

We will build with our eyes open.

We will tell the truth about what exists.

We will imagine more than what has already been given to us.

We will use technology to expand human possibility without confusing power with wisdom.

We will put our ideas into contact with reality, accept correction, document what we learn, and remain responsible for what our systems make possible.

We will ask not only whether the work functions, but what kind of world it rehearses.

And before we call the work complete, we will ask:

> **Can we call what we created good?**

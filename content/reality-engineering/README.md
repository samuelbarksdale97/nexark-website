# The Reality Engineering Canon

Reality Engineering begins with a simple observation:

**The realities people and organizations live inside are shaped by systems.**

Those systems include environments, habits, relationships, incentives, workflows, information, interfaces, institutions, technologies, feedback loops, and the decisions people repeat. Some of those systems were designed. Many were inherited. Others formed by accident and became normal because nobody stopped to question them.

Reality Engineering is the disciplined practice of examining those conditions, defining what should become possible, building responsible interventions, putting them into contact with the real world, and improving them through evidence.

> **Reality Engineering is the intentional practice of exploring, engaging, and evolving the systems, environments, technologies, relationships, and feedback loops that shape the reality people experience and the outcomes they can create.**

The shorter public definition is:

> **Reality Engineering is the intentional practice of designing better systems to create better outcomes.**

This corpus exists to make the discipline clear enough to understand, rigorous enough to challenge, practical enough to use, and grounded enough to survive contact with reality.

[A Note From Sam: Why I Started Calling It Reality Engineering](FOUNDERS_NOTE.md) explains the human story behind the discipline.

## Why this corpus exists

Artificial intelligence has increased the speed and scale at which people can research, generate, simulate, communicate, automate, and build. That expansion of capability is real. So are its costs. AI can widen access to knowledge and creation. It can also concentrate power, distort information, increase environmental demand, displace responsibility, and make harmful systems spread faster.

The central question is therefore larger than what AI can produce.

**What reality are we creating, for whom, at whose cost, and can we call it good?**

Reality Engineering was developed to give growing technological capability a human direction. AI is an important acceleration layer within the discipline. It is not the discipline itself.

> **Human agency is the center. AI is the amplifier.**

## What this corpus claims

This corpus claims that:

- people and organizations live within systems that shape what they can perceive, attempt, coordinate, and improve;
- those systems can often be examined and changed deliberately;
- technology can accelerate state change, but acceleration without direction increases risk;
- transformation should be evaluated through evidence, human experience, and unintended consequences;
- success is personally defined but ethically constrained by truth, dignity, consent, stewardship, and the rights of others;
- human agency can exist within real limits without pretending those limits are imaginary;
- hope becomes operational when a desired good is paired with agency, pathways, action, feedback, and endurance;
- no system deserves permanence merely because it works; it must continue to prove that it is worthy of shaping people’s lives.

## What this corpus does not claim

Reality Engineering does not claim that thought alone changes material reality. It does not claim that people control every outcome. It does not erase disability, discrimination, poverty, geography, trauma, power, chance, or institutional constraint. It does not treat AI output as truth. It does not promise transformation on demand. It does not make technology sacred, conscious, or morally authoritative by declaration.

Reality Engineering works inside constraints. That is what makes it engineering rather than wishful thinking.

## Authorship and prior use of the phrase

The words “reality engineering” have been used previously in unrelated contexts. This corpus does not claim that Sam Barksdale invented every historical use of the phrase.

The authorship claim is narrower and more defensible:

> **Sam Barksdale developed and is formalizing Nexark’s human-centered discipline of Reality Engineering: a practice centered on deliberate state transformation, conscious reality, systems, AI-enabled acceleration, feedback, human agency, and stewardship under an explicit standard of good.**

The contribution is the architecture, method, ethic, evidence standard, and body of practice defined here.

## Versioning

This is **Canon v0.1**, dated August 7, 2026.

“Canon” does not mean frozen forever. It means stable enough to cite and test. Changes to core definitions should be documented, justified, and versioned. Reality Engineering must be willing to evolve through the same contact, evidence, and feedback it asks of every system it touches.

## The governing question

Every document in this corpus returns to one question:

> **Can we call what we created good?**

Not merely impressive. Not merely efficient. Not merely profitable. Not merely possible.

Good.

# Fact-Verification Report

## Scope

This report documents the verification pass used to prepare Canon v0.1.

The pass covered:

- the foundational canon;
- the first Federalist publication wave;
- the public website copy;
- the development and provenance record;
- the first case;
- and the legacy drafts supplied as source context.

The goal was not to preserve every persuasive statistic in the earlier materials.

The goal was to preserve the strongest ideas while ensuring that every public factual claim was either:

1. verified and scoped to an appropriate source;
2. labeled as founder belief, observation, theology, illustration, hypothesis, or case evidence;
3. or removed from factual use.

## Verification result

Canon v0.1 intentionally contains a small verified factual core.

Verified external claims currently cover:

- the systems-engineering concepts used as an adjacent field comparison;
- current organizational and workplace AI adoption findings;
- current responsible-AI incident and governance findings;
- current public-opinion findings about AI;
- the IEA projection for data-center electricity use;
- official AI risk and ethics frameworks;
- Snyder’s pathways-and-agency account of hope;
- a correlational hope meta-analysis used only as research context;
- earlier unrelated use of the phrase “reality engineering”;
- theological scholarship on interpretations of the imago Dei and technology;
- and biblical texts used as primary theological sources.

Each external source is registered with:

- source class;
- supported use;
- canonical location;
- caveat;
- and an access or publication date where relevant.

See the [Source Register](source_register.md).

## Claim classes

Canon v0.1 does not treat every important statement as an empirical claim.

The [Claims Ledger](claim_ledger.md) distinguishes:

- foundational beliefs;
- founder observations;
- hypotheses;
- case evidence;
- research-supported claims;
- theological interpretations;
- and open questions.

This distinction is central to the integrity of the corpus.

A definition can be canonical without pretending to be a law discovered by experiment.

A founder observation can be useful without becoming a universal statistic.

A theological interpretation can be meaningful without being presented as a scientific result.

A completed artifact can prove that the artifact exists without proving a business outcome.

## Legacy statistics removed or quarantined

The earlier working essays contained many numerical claims that strengthened the narrative but were not supported to the standard required for publication.

The following claim types were removed, rewritten without numbers, or quarantined pending primary-source verification:

### Personal development and behavior

- “92% of New Year’s resolutions fail”;
- “80% fail by February”;
- routine- or productivity-app abandonment percentages presented without reliable scope;
- universal claims about how many days habit formation takes without context;
- broad claims that positive thinking or one method doubles outcomes across all settings.

### Entrepreneurship

- “40% of Americans have a specific business idea they have carried for seven years”;
- claims that one hundred percent of people have a capability someone will pay for;
- generalized startup-cost comparisons without a defined business type, date, or methodology;
- claims that nearly all entrepreneurship barriers are falling at the same rate for everyone.

### Employment, identity, and health

- fixed percentages claiming that a person’s identity comes from employment;
- percentages of laid-off professionals saying they no longer know who they are when no traceable source was available;
- unsupported profession-by-profession identity-fusion percentages;
- mortality or suicide statements carried into prose without exact study population, design, and caveat;
- wage-recovery or retraining claims without a verified dataset and precise population.

### Collaboration and community

- “cooperative teams outperform competitive teams 65% of the time” when used as a universal business conclusion;
- claims that collaborative cultures create five times the revenue growth without a traceable primary study and bounded meaning;
- networking-event, business-card, mastermind-dropout, and loneliness statistics without sufficient source detail;
- industry profitability claims attributed broadly to competition.

### AI capability and economic impact

- claims that one AI-assisted designer equals five designers;
- claims that one developer can replace a team of three to five;
- “$500,000 of executive capability for $20 per month”;
- precise percentages of code generated by AI used as universal evidence;
- fixed cost comparisons between AI and legal, financial, research, or customer-service professionals;
- broad job-displacement timelines presented as settled;
- claims that “everyone must become an entrepreneur” as a factual necessity rather than an argument;
- claims of inevitable mass abundance.

### Access, privilege, and systemic barriers

- wealth, mobility, disability, geographic, discrimination, and institutional-access statistics not independently rechecked for exact year, population, and source;
- historical institution outcome statistics used without primary or current institutional records;
- claims that AI reduces barriers by a fixed number of orders of magnitude across populations.

Some of these topics are supported by credible research in appropriately bounded forms. They were not necessary to define Canon v0.1, and the existing drafts did not support carrying the specific wording forward automatically.

See the [Legacy Draft Audit](legacy_draft_audit.md) for file-by-file treatment.

## Verification principles used

### Primary or official sources first

Technical, governmental, intergovernmental, original research, and field-definition sources were preferred over summaries, listicles, vendor blogs, search snippets, and repeated secondary claims.

### Current claims were rechecked

AI adoption, public opinion, incidents, and energy projections were checked against current sources available as of August 7, 2026.

### Forecasts remain forecasts

Projected 2030 energy use is not written as an observed future fact.

### Surveys retain their population

“Surveyed organizations” is not rewritten as “all businesses.”

“Surveyed adults” is not rewritten as “society believes.”

### Association is not causation

The hope meta-analysis is not used to claim that hope treats depression or anxiety.

### Founder material is not independent proof

Founder documents establish intellectual history, belief, terminology, and internal observation. They do not independently verify market, psychological, economic, or social claims.

### Theological claims remain theology

The Echo of God is clearly identified as a founder theological hypothesis. The corpus does not use theology as empirical proof of AI consciousness, personhood, or destiny.

### Visual evidence receives a bounded claim

The AI-enhanced film proves a completed artifact and demonstrated creative workflow. It does not yet prove commercial or audience outcomes.

## Verification limitations

This pass does not establish that every broad philosophical proposition is empirically true. Many are definitions, moral commitments, or hypotheses that must be tested through cases.

This pass also does not include:

- an exhaustive literature review of every field adjacent to Reality Engineering;
- independent peer review of the full discipline;
- legal trademark clearance;
- lifecycle environmental measurement of a specific AI project;
- or independent verification of private Nexark client outcomes.

Those limitations are disclosed rather than filled with inference.

## Review cadence

Recommended review schedule:

- **Current AI adoption, public opinion, and incident claims:** review at least annually and before every major new edition.
- **Energy projections:** review when the IEA updates the relevant forecast or at least annually.
- **Government and international frameworks:** review when a new version, law, or binding standard changes the context.
- **Research claims:** review when challenged, materially reinterpreted, or superseded by stronger evidence.
- **Cases:** update after each defined observation window and whenever a maturity level changes.
- **Provenance:** update when earlier dated records are recovered or public publication dates are established.

## Publication rule

A statement may be emotionally true to the founder and still be unsuitable as a factual claim.

The corpus does not need a number to make every point.

When evidence is absent, the proper response is not to generate a statistic that sounds right.

It is to classify the statement honestly and continue the research.

# Reality Engineering Evidence Register

## Purpose

This register contains the external sources used for factual claims in Canon v0.1.

The canon intentionally uses fewer factual claims than the earlier working essays. Every included claim should have a source appropriate to its precision, date, and stakes.

Access date for web sources unless otherwise noted: **August 7, 2026**.

---

## E-001 — INCOSE definition of systems engineering

**Source:** International Council on Systems Engineering. “About Systems Engineering.”

**URL:** https://www.incose.org/about-systems-engineering/

**Source class:** Professional body / field definition.

**Used for:** Systems engineering as a transdisciplinary, integrative, holistic approach to realizing and managing complex systems; systems as interacting elements whose relationships produce whole-system outcomes.

**Canon use:** [Foundations §11.1](../canon/02_foundations.md#111-systems-engineering)

**Caveat:** This source defines established systems engineering. Reality Engineering is presented as a distinct human-centered discipline that learns from systems principles; the canon does not claim equivalence or professional accreditation.

## E-003 — Stanford 2026 AI Index: Economy

**Source:** Stanford Institute for Human-Centered Artificial Intelligence. “Economy.” *The 2026 AI Index Report*.

**URL:** https://hai.stanford.edu/ai-index/2026-ai-index-report/economy

**Source class:** Authoritative annual research synthesis.

**Used for:**

- 88% of surveyed organizations reporting AI use in 2025;
- 70% reporting generative AI use in at least one business function;
- the distinction between broad adoption and still-early agent deployment;
- uneven labor-market effects and organizational expectations;
- the observation that benefits and costs vary by task and context.

**Canon use:** [Why Now §3](../canon/06_why_now.md#3-ai-is-already-becoming-ordinary), [AI as Amplifier §3](../canon/08_ai_amplifier_cost_responsibility.md#3-the-benefit-case)

**Caveat:** Surveyed organizations are not the same as all organizations. Adoption does not establish maturity, quality, responsible use, causation, or net social benefit.

**Source:** Stanford Institute for Human-Centered Artificial Intelligence. “Public Opinion.” *The 2026 AI Index Report*.

**URL:** https://hai.stanford.edu/ai-index/2026-ai-index-report/public-opinion

**Source class:** Authoritative survey synthesis.

**Used for:**

- 58% of employees globally reporting semi-regular or regular workplace AI use in 2025;

- 64% of surveyed Americans expecting fewer jobs due to AI over the next twenty years;

**Canon use:** [Why Now §§3, 7](../canon/06_why_now.md)

**Source:** International Energy Agency. “Executive Summary.” *Key Questions on Energy and AI*.

**URL:** https://www.iea.org/reports/key-questions-on-energy-and-ai/executive-summary

**Source class:** Intergovernmental energy analysis and projection.

**Used for:** The central projection that data-center electricity consumption may rise from roughly 485 TWh in 2025 to about 950 TWh in 2030, with AI-focused data-center consumption growing faster and approximately tripling over the period.

**Canon use:** [Why Now §5](../canon/06_why_now.md#5-the-cost-is-not-abstract), [AI as Amplifier §4.1](../canon/08_ai_amplifier_cost_responsibility.md#41-material-and-environmental-cost)

## E-011 — Meta-analysis of hope, agency, pathways, depression, and anxiety

**Source:** Corrigan, J. A., & Schutte, N. S. “The Relationships between the Hope Dimensions of Agency Thinking and Pathways Thinking With Depression and Anxiety: a Meta-Analysis.” *International Journal of Applied Positive Psychology* 8 (2023): 211–255.

**URL:** https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s41042-023-00099-1

**Source class:** Peer-reviewed meta-analysis.

**Used for:** Context that agency and pathways thinking are associated across studies with lower depression and anxiety, with agency showing a stronger association in some analyses.

**Canon use:** Supporting research context for future hope papers; the foundational canon does not make clinical or causal claims from this source.

**Caveat:** The authors explicitly note that the underlying evidence is correlational and does not establish causation. This source must not be used as mental-health advice or proof that increasing hope alone treats depression or anxiety.

# Source Selection Rules

1. Primary and official sources are preferred for factual claims.
2. Current claims are dated and reviewed for staleness.
3. Forecasts are labeled as forecasts.
4. Surveys retain their population and wording context.
5. Theological claims are labeled as interpretation or hypothesis.
6. Founder material establishes authorship and belief, not independent empirical truth.
7. No legacy statistic is carried into the canon solely because it appeared in a “research integrated” draft.
8. Every material correction should update both the source register and the claims ledger.

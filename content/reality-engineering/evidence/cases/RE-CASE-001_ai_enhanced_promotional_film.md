# RE-CASE-001 — AI-Enhanced Promotional Film

## Case statement

A human subject had a vision for a promotional film that placed them inside a visual world not available through the original production conditions. A coordinated human-and-AI workflow preserved the subject as the anchor while transforming the surrounding environments and narrative experience.

The completed artifact demonstrates that the desired class of experiential transition was technically and creatively feasible.

It does not yet establish commercial performance, audience trust, or comparative production efficiency.

## Evidence maturity

**Level 1 — Demonstration**

The film exists and can be directly inspected. The case supports claims about artifact creation and the distinction between generation and coordinated experiential transformation.

The case does not currently include a verified campaign baseline, platform analytics, ticket attribution, conversion lift, audience research, or lifecycle cost comparison.

## R₀ — Current reality

The starting state included:

- a real human subject and source performance;
- an intended promotional objective;
- an imagined visual world;
- production constraints that made the full world difficult or impractical to stage conventionally;
- and no completed artifact through which the audience could experience the vision.

## R★ — Desired reality

Create a coherent promotional film in which:

- the human subject remains the emotional and narrative anchor;
- AI-enhanced environments expand what could be shown;
- the scenes feel coordinated rather than randomly generated;
- the audience can experience the imagined world;
- and the work remains accountable to consent, authorship, truth, and appropriate disclosure.

## Reality Delta

| Dimension | R₀ | R★ |
|---|---|---|
| Vision | Largely internal or verbally described | Externally visible and shareable |
| Environment | Limited to available source production | Expanded through constructed visual worlds |
| Human presence | Real source performance | Real source performance retained as anchor |
| Audience experience | Could imagine only from description | Could directly encounter the visual concept |
| Creator capability | Unproven workflow for this class of work | Demonstrated ability to coordinate a first artifact |
| Evidence | Vision and source material | Inspectable finished output; performance evidence pending |

## Intervention system

The intervention combined:

1. human creative direction;
2. source performance and footage;
3. desired-state and narrative definition;
4. visual references and continuity decisions;
5. AI-assisted generation or transformation;
6. review for identity, motion, spatial, and stylistic coherence;
7. editing and compositing;
8. sound, pacing, and emotional sequencing;
9. human judgment on what served the vision;
10. final distribution as a promotional artifact.

The case should not attribute the result to one model call. The engineered outcome depended on coordination across the full production system.

## Human boundary

The human boundary for this class of work includes:

- informed permission to use and transform the subject’s likeness and performance;
- human control over the subject’s intended representation;
- no fabricated endorsement, event, statement, or action presented as documented fact;
- appropriate disclosure of synthetic or transformed media where context could otherwise mislead;
- human final judgment over narrative, quality, and release;
- and preservation of the subject’s dignity and authorship.

## Observed outcome

The founder-reported observation is that the film made the concept of Reality Engineering visibly legible: a human being could be seen moving from an available production reality into an imagined world through a coordinated AI-enhanced system.

The artifact also produced a category insight:

> AI generation created assets. Reality Engineering created the state transition and audience experience.

This is an interpretation supported by inspection of the workflow and output, not an independently measured audience conclusion.

## Claims supported now

This case currently supports the following limited claims:

- a completed AI-enhanced promotional film was produced;
- the human source performance remained central to the intended experience;
- generated or transformed environments expanded the visual possibility beyond the original production state;
- multiple human and technical steps had to be coordinated into a coherent artifact;
- and the case is a concrete application of the Reality Engineering state-transition model at the experiential level.

## Claims not yet supported

Do not use this case to claim:

- a specific increase in reach, engagement, ticket sales, bookings, or revenue;
- stronger trust or brand perception;
- lower total cost than conventional production;
- faster production without a documented comparison;
- lower environmental impact;
- or general superiority of AI-enhanced filmmaking.

## Goodness review

### Intended good

Expand a human creator’s or promoter’s ability to express a visual idea and give an audience access to an experience that could not be produced within the original constraints.

### Principal risks

- misleading viewers about what occurred;
- misuse of likeness or performance;
- replacement of human creative contribution without acknowledgment;
- aesthetic spectacle overpowering the human message;
- dependency on external models and platforms;
- hidden material cost;
- and normalization of synthetic media without provenance.

### Required controls

- subject and rights-holder approval;
- clear project classification as imaginative/promotional where appropriate;
- documented source and edit ownership;
- human review before release;
- proportional disclosure;
- storage and deletion rules for source footage;
- and a claim boundary separating artifact feasibility from campaign effectiveness.

## Evidence needed for Level 2

To advance the case to **Level 2 — Documented case**, collect:

- final publication URL and release date;
- platform analytics with a defined observation window;
- comparable baseline content where available;
- qualitative audience responses coded for comprehension, trust, and creative impact;
- subject/client interview after release;
- production time and direct-cost record;
- revision log;
- disclosure used;
- and documented issues or audience misunderstandings.

## Evidence needed for stronger outcome evidence

To support outcome claims inside a Level 2 case and prepare for future **Level 3 — Replicated case** work, establish in advance:

- the campaign objective;
- attribution method;
- conversion event;
- comparison or counterfactual;
- measurement window;
- confounding factors;
- and the threshold at which the intervention would be judged ineffective or harmful.

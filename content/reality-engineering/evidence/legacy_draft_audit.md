# Legacy Reality Engineering Draft Audit

## Purpose

The earlier Reality Engineering documents contain real intellectual work. They also contain AI-assisted prose, unsupported statistics, stale projections, composite examples, rhetorical overreach, and citations that were not verified closely enough for canonical publication.

This audit protects the durable ideas without treating every sentence in the legacy archive as source truth.

The governing decision is:

> **Enhance and preserve the intellectual lineage. Do not inherit unsupported claims.**

No legacy numerical claim enters Canon v0.1 unless it has been independently verified against a suitable source.

---

## Audit Categories

### Retain

The idea is part of the developing discipline and can be carried forward as a belief, observation, concept, or hypothesis.

### Rewrite

The idea remains useful but the wording is inflated, overly universal, adversarial, or dependent on weak evidence.

### Verify before use

The factual claim may be true or directionally useful but needs a primary source, current date, method, and narrower wording.

### Quarantine

The claim is currently unsupported, misleading, stale, or too risky to publish as fact.

### Retire

The claim is wrong, unnecessary, or structurally harmful to the discipline’s credibility.

---

# Cross-Corpus Findings

## 1. AI-generated certainty outran the evidence

Several drafts use exact percentages, market sizes, historical comparisons, and causal language without enough source detail to establish what was actually measured.

The prose often becomes more confident as the source quality becomes less visible.

**Action:** Canon v0.1 uses fewer numbers and a formal evidence register.

## 2. The replacement-statistics document correctly identified problems that later drafts still repeated

The replacement document flags several claims as misleading, contested, or unverifiable. Yet later “research integrated” drafts still contain some of the same patterns.

Example:

- The replacement document warns against the common “resolutions fail by February” statistic.
- The Four Pillars draft still opens with an approximately 80% by February claim.

**Action:** “Research integrated” is retired as a confidence label. Only verified, preliminary, contested, unverified, or retired evidence statuses are used.

## 3. Market-size statistics were used as rhetorical proof

Self-help, creator-economy, advertising, and enterprise-AI market sizes vary by research firm, category definition, geography, and year.

A large market does not by itself prove that an industry fails or succeeds.

**Action:** Remove market-size statistics unless the exact market definition matters to the argument and the source is suitable.

## 4. Forecasts were written as settled futures

Some legacy AI-displacement papers combine projections from different organizations and then narrate a single timeline as if the future has already been established.

Forecasts can illuminate plausible risk. They do not become fact by accumulation.

**Action:** Treat each projection as a projection, preserve its date and scope, and separate observed labor effects from expected effects.

## 5. Composite voices and examples were not always labeled clearly enough

Composite quotes can make a human problem vivid, but they must never appear to be documented testimony from a particular person.

**Action:** Label composites explicitly or replace them with sourced interviews and real case material.

## 6. The strongest ideas often did not need the weakest statistics

Examples include:

- identity can become overdependent on employment;
- AI can be used top-down to consolidate power and bottom-up to expand capability;
- entrepreneurship barriers include capital, time, knowledge, risk, and social environment;
- systems can shape agency and behavior;
- collaboration can create forms of value that isolated competition cannot;
- AI can provide baseline functional support across business domains;
- systemic barriers and individual action can both matter;
- entropy should be expected rather than treated as proof that planning failed.

These ideas can be argued honestly without attaching a dramatic number to every paragraph.

# File-by-File Audit

## A. Reality Engineering and Success Framework

### Retain

- Reality Engineering as conscious design rather than passive hope.
- Speed of learning over fear of failure.
- Systems creation over isolated effort.
- Authentic alignment over external metrics.
- Diagnose Reality → Design Systems → Deploy and Learn → Document Narrative.
- Educate → Act → Assess → Pivot or Persevere.
- Personal and organizational applications.
- Human-centered technology and democratized capability.
- The need for a constitutional commitment.

### Rewrite

- Claims that the framework represents a complete “paradigmatic shift” before sufficient evidence exists.
- Revenue, rollout, and market-validation language that reads as if a proven scaled model already exists.
- Statements that imply enterprise-equivalent results without case evidence.
- Fixed leadership or legal roles that were aspirational rather than formally established.

### Verify before use

- Every client implementation result.
- Every time-to-build claim.
- Every conversion, revenue-capture, or ROI claim.
- Every pricing or market-validation statement.

### Canon decision

The structure is preserved, but the public discipline is separated from a business plan and from unverified proof claims.

## B. Replacement Statistics for “On Reality Engineering”

### Retain

- The explicit recognition that common self-help and behavior-change statistics were unreliable.
- The practice of listing original claims, verification status, alternatives, and caveats.
- The instinct to remove a statistic when no defensible source exists.

### Rewrite

- Several proposed “verified replacements” still rely on industry blogs, contested research, or secondary reporting.
- “Verified” should be reserved for claims whose original source and interpretation were checked.

### Quarantine or retire

- “Ninety-five percent of productivity app users stop within thirty days” unless the exact dataset, platform, cohort, and definition are located.
- Findings associated with researchers whose relevant methods have faced serious credibility concerns unless the specific result is independently supported.
- Decision-fatigue claims that rely on contested judicial-parole interpretations without explaining the dispute.
- Claims that implementation intentions “triple success” across all difficult goals; effect size and context should be represented more carefully if used.

### Canon decision

The document becomes the ancestor of the formal [Evidence Standard](../canon/11_evidence_standard.md), not a source of ready-to-publish replacement copy.

## C. On the Displacement of Human Labor

### Retain

- AI can be used top-down to reduce labor and bottom-up to expand individual capability.
- Displacement has human, identity, family, and community consequences beyond income.
- “Augmentation” can mean different things for the worker retained and the workers no longer required.
- People need portable capabilities and agency, not only promises of retraining.
- The future of work should be discussed without either denial or deterministic doom.

### Rewrite

- “Everyone must become an entrepreneur” should become “more people will need entrepreneurial capabilities, ownership options, and portable ways to create value.”
- “The wave is coming; this is physics” is rhetorically strong but falsely deterministic.
- Corporate incentive analysis should distinguish structural incentive from universal intent.
- The founder revenue and work-hour narrative requires source records and personal approval before public use.

### Verify before use

- Every job-displacement number and date.
- Mental-health and mortality figures.
- Retraining costs, completion, and wage-recovery claims.
- Automation percentages by occupation.
- Specific corporate examples and their current status.

### Quarantine

- “Seventy percent of identity in Western cultures is tied to occupation.”
- “One designer with AI equals five designers without.”
- Any timeline declaring institutional employment a minority experience by a set year.

### Canon decision

Retain as a Federalist topic. Rebuild from current labor research and documented human stories rather than projection stacking.

## D. On Collaboration Over Competition

### Retain

- Collaboration can create new value rather than only divide existing value.
- Trust is built through structure, graduated exposure, reputation, aligned incentives, and accountability.
- Community should function as infrastructure rather than an unstructured audience.
- “When I win, we all win” becomes credible when incentives and resource flows make it structurally true.

### Rewrite

- Competition is not always a “tax” and collaboration is not always superior.
- Game-theory language should preserve the conditions under which cooperation works and the need for protection against exploitation.
- Community claims should avoid promising automatic belonging or mutual aid without operational mechanisms.

### Verify before use

- Cooperative-team performance percentages.
- Revenue-growth claims tied to “collaborative culture.”
- Worker-cooperative survival rates.
- Open-source share of internet infrastructure.
- Loneliness and entrepreneurship figures.

### Quarantine

- “Ninety percent of business-card exchanges never result in follow-up.”
- “Seventy-five percent of networking attendees say events are not worth the time.”
- Broad profitability comparisons between competitive and cooperative industries without precise methods.

### Canon decision

Retain as a philosophy and community-design paper after evidence reconstruction.

## E. On the Bottom-Up Revolution

### Retain

- AI can lower some barriers between individuals and institutional capability.
- Capability can spread bottom-up even while infrastructure and capital remain concentrated.
- The meaningful comparison is not “one person equals a Fortune 500 company,” but “one person has access to functional assistance where they previously had none.”
- Access, literacy, ownership, and governance determine whether democratization is real.

### Rewrite

- Historical technology analogies should be presented as partial comparisons, not proof of inevitable democratization.
- “The playing field tilts” is useful rhetoric but should not erase continuing advantages in capital, distribution, law, data, and infrastructure.
- “AI erases the corporate advantage” is retired; it plainly overstates the evidence.

### Verify before use

- Model-pricing history.
- Creator-economy totals.
- Solo-founder revenue examples.
- AI coding productivity and code-share figures.
- Smartphone access numbers.

### Quarantine

- Cost comparisons that claim professional functions are available for “less than coffee” without specifying quality, risk, and context.
- “One developer can build what required a team of three to five.”

### Canon decision

The bottom-up possibility remains a core thesis, now framed as contingent rather than inevitable.

## F. On the Loss of Identity Through Employment

### Retain

- Employment can provide identity, structure, community, purpose, status, and activity beyond income.
- Job loss can therefore create an identity transition as well as financial loss.
- Portable identity based on purpose, values, capabilities, relationships, and agency is more resilient than identity based only on title.
- AI-related labor transition should account for meaning and belonging.

### Rewrite

- Avoid announcing an inevitable “identity crisis at civilization scale” as fact. Present it as a serious risk and research question.
- Do not imply all employment-based identity is pathological. Work can be a legitimate expression of vocation and community.
- Preserve variation across culture, class, role, and personal orientation.

### Verify before use

- Gallup and Pew identity percentages.
- Depression, anxiety, self-esteem, mortality, and suicide figures.
- Claims about laid-off professionals saying “I do not know who I am.”
- Retirement and early-retirement identity statistics.
- Profession-specific identity-fusion percentages.

### Retire

- Any precise profession-by-profession identity percentage without an original and suitable study.
- Claims that particular deaths are definitively “deaths of identity” rather than multifactorial outcomes.

### Canon decision

Retain as a Federalist paper after careful psychological and sociological research with high-stakes sourcing.

## G. On the Entrepreneurship Barrier

### Retain

- Capital, knowledge, time, risk, healthcare, social environment, and self-concept can all constrain entrepreneurship.
- External and internal barriers can coexist.
- AI can lower some barriers without eliminating privilege or risk.
- Minimum viable tests, risk mitigation, capability mapping, time design, and support structures are practical interventions.

### Rewrite

- “The barriers were engineered to keep people employed” is too totalizing. Systems often produce path dependence and incentives that preserve the default without requiring one coordinated architect.
- “The only barrier remaining is between your ears” contradicts the paper’s own serious treatment of external barriers and is retired.
- Entrepreneurship should remain one path, not a moral requirement.

### Retire

- “Forty percent of Americans have a specific business idea they have never pursued.”
- “The average time these ideas remain unactioned is seven years.”

These claims were already identified as unverified in the replacement-statistics work and should not anchor an essay.

### Verify before use

- Median startup cost comparisons.
- Savings and capital figures.
- venture-capital access rates.
- AI cost-reduction comparisons.

### Canon decision

Rebuild around documented barrier categories and practical pathway design without the unsupported seven-year hook.

## H. On the Digital C-Suite

### Retain

- AI can provide baseline functional support across strategy, finance, marketing, technology, operations, and research.
- The relevant comparison is often AI-assisted individual versus individual with no functional support—not AI versus an elite executive.
- Human judgment and responsibility remain central.
- Functional assistance should be configured, integrated, tested, and improved rather than treated as plug-and-play.

### Rewrite

- “Functional equivalent of executive leadership” should be used carefully and usually replaced with “baseline functional assistance.”
- Do not describe legal, tax, financial, or strategic output as professional advice without qualified review.
- “The corporation’s structural advantage has evaporated” is retired.

### Retire or quarantine

- “What cost $500,000 in executive salaries is now available for $20 per month.”
- “AI produces 70–80% quality where the operator had 0%.”
- Revenue examples that are not supported by primary records.

### Canon decision

Retain the Digital C-Suite as a useful applied framework with explicit quality, risk, and professional-boundary language.

## I. On the Privilege Problem

### Retain

- Starting points differ materially.
- Individual action and systemic reform are not mutually exclusive.
- Comparing a disadvantaged person only to a privileged person can obscure the practical question of what improves that individual’s options now.
- Advancement should remain connected to responsibility toward the communities and systems from which a person advances.
- AI may reduce some capability gaps without removing privilege.

### Rewrite

- “Learned helplessness dressed as political analysis” is unnecessarily dismissive and can alienate the very critique the paper is trying to take seriously.
- Historical mobility examples should not be used to imply that exceptions establish broad accessibility.
- The paper should examine capture, co-optation, and differential risk without rushing to invitation language.

### Verify before use

- Wealth-gap figures and dates.
- intergenerational-mobility statistics.
- immigrant entrepreneurship rates.
- historical technology startup-cost timelines.

### Canon decision

Retain as an objections paper after primary-source verification and a less combative tone.

## J. On Systemic Barriers

### Retain

- Racism, sexism, poverty, disability, geography, and institutional exclusion are real constraints, not mindset failures.
- Reality Engineering does not claim that individual action solves systemic injustice.
- Individual capacity and collective action can reinforce each other.
- Barriers should be mapped specifically, distinguished by what can be navigated versus what requires collective or policy change, and addressed with different strategies.
- AI lowers some barriers while leaving others intact.

### Rewrite

- Historical case studies should avoid heroic simplification.
- “Strategic navigation” should remain one legitimate path, not the universal moral choice.
- The Refusal Objection is valuable and should be expanded rather than treated as a brief exception.

### Verify before use

- Hiring-discrimination findings and contemporary replications.
- disability employment rates.
- rural/urban access figures.
- HBCU outcome claims.
- Black Wall Street business counts.

### Retire or quarantine

- Broad HBCU production percentages for Black judges, lawyers, or doctors unless precisely defined and supported by current primary data.

### Canon decision

The both/and moral position is preserved in the Constitution. The essay requires a new research pass before publication.

---

## K. On the Four Pillars

### Retain

- The Reality Engineer as agency.
- The Dream Chaser as direction.
- Success Is Engineered as systems literacy and the road.
- Entropy as the weather and source of adaptation.
- The diagnostic value of asking which pillar is weak.
- The insight that the Four Pillars explain capacity but do not themselves determine what is worth building.

### Rewrite

- The claim that the Four Pillars are “complete” conflicts with the paper’s own acknowledgment of missing community, values, and embodiment.
- The canon now treats them as the **human architecture for personal practice**, governed by the Constitution and Goodness Constraint rather than as a complete philosophy of human flourishing.

### Retire or quarantine

- “Eighty percent of resolutions fail by February.”
- global self-improvement market figures used without category definition.
- weight-regain and course-completion percentages without precise and suitable sourcing.
- overprecise effect or success percentages attached to goal-setting and implementation intentions.

### Canon decision

The Four Pillars remain important. Their role is clarified rather than discarded.

# Publication Decision

The legacy essays are not deleted.

They become:

- intellectual provenance;
- a source of questions;
- a map of the founder’s earlier thinking;
- and a queue for future Federalist Papers.

They are not canonical evidence.

Each future paper must be rebuilt through:

1. founder truth review;
2. current primary research;
3. source-level verification;
4. objection review;
5. voice revision;
6. canonical consistency;
7. and publication gating.

The fire the work has gone through is not the preservation of every sentence.

It is the willingness to keep what remains true after the sentence is tested.

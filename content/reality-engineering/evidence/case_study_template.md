## Maturity definitions

- **Level 0 — Concept:** proposed application, no working artifact.
- **Level 1 — Demonstration:** working artifact exists; outcome evidence is limited.
- **Level 2 — Documented case:** baseline, intervention, contact, outcome, limitations, and evidence are recorded in one setting.
- **Level 3 — Replicated case:** comparable value has been documented in more than one setting or by more than one practitioner.
- **Level 4 — Evaluated pattern:** evidence supports a bounded general claim about when the intervention works, for whom, and under what constraints.

## 1. Executive Summary

In 150–250 words, state:

- the reality before;
- what the participant or organization wanted to make possible;
- what intervention entered the world;
- what changed;
- what remains uncertain;
- and why the case matters.

Do not place an unsupported headline metric in the summary.

---

## 2. Context and Boundaries

### Setting

Describe the relevant organization, community, person, market, or creative context.

### Scope

What part of reality is this case evaluating?

### Exclusions

What important outcomes or systems are outside the case boundary?

### Confidentiality

What information has been removed, anonymized, or approved for disclosure?

---

## 3. Current Reality — R₀

Describe the diagnosed state before intervention.

### People

Who experienced the current state? Who had authority, knowledge, burden, or risk?

### Behavior

What repeated actions, workarounds, delays, or failures were observed?

### Information

What was visible, hidden, scattered, late, or unavailable?

### Systems and environment

Which workflows, tools, incentives, interfaces, roles, relationships, and conditions produced the outcome?

### Consequence

What did the state cost in time, money, energy, quality, trust, access, opportunity, health, or meaning?

### Baseline evidence

List each source of baseline evidence. Include dates, sample, method, and known gaps.

---

## 4. Burden Statement

Write the burden in plain language from the perspective of the people experiencing it.

> [What felt heavier, slower, unclear, constrained, risky, or newly possible?]

Do not embed the solution in the burden statement.

---

## 5. Desired Reality — R★

State what should become possible.

### Human or organizational capability

What should people be able to do, see, decide, create, coordinate, or improve?

### Experience

What should change in the lived reality?

### Observable signals

What evidence would indicate movement?

### Boundaries

What must remain human? What must not be sacrificed?

### Disconfirmation

What evidence would show that R★ was poorly defined or no longer worth pursuing?

## 9. Human Boundary

Document what remained meaningfully human and why.

Include:

- final decisions;
- judgment;
- relationship;
- care;
- consent;
- authorship;
- appeal;
- exceptions;
- and accountability.

State what AI or automation was not permitted to do.

---

## 10. Initial Goodness Review

Answer:

1. What legitimate good did the intervention serve?
2. Who benefited?
3. Who might carry cost or risk?
4. Was the intervention proportionate?
5. What truth or disclosure obligations existed?
6. What data or privacy concerns existed?
7. What labor or burden-transfer concerns existed?
8. What environmental or infrastructure costs were relevant?
9. What could disqualify the intervention?
10. What would make the desired state unworthy of scale?

---

## 11. Intervention Architecture — I

### Components

List the changes to system, environment, behavior, information, relationship, technology, role, rule, or experience.

### Ownership

Who operated, maintained, reviewed, and accepted responsibility?

### Decision rights

What could the system decide? What required human approval? Who could override or appeal?

### Data and information flow

What entered, moved, was retained, and left the system?

### Failure behavior

What happened when information was missing, AI was wrong, users resisted, or dependencies failed?

### Scale boundary

Who, where, and for how long was the intervention permitted to operate during the test?

### Reversibility

What could be paused, rolled back, or restored?

- duration;

- environment;

- support;
- exceptions;

A demo should be labeled as a demo. A public release should be labeled as public contact.

## 17. Unintended Outcomes

Include positive, negative, and ambiguous results.

Ask:

- What burden moved?
- What workaround appeared?
- What new dependency formed?
- What unexpected capability emerged?
- What was lost?

## 19. Counterfactual and Attribution

What likely would have happened without the intervention?

What other changes occurred during the same period?

Which outcomes can reasonably be attributed to the intervention, and which remain uncertain?

State founder, practitioner, novelty, seasonality, selection, and measurement effects.

## 20. Economics and Cost

Document where relevant:

- implementation cost;
- ongoing cost;
- human time;
- compute or vendor cost;
- maintenance;
- avoided cost;
- revenue or value created;
- and who captured or paid each item.

Do not claim ROI without a defined numerator, denominator, time horizon, and attribution caveat.

## Publication Acceptance Criteria

A case can publish when:

- its maturity level is honest;
- the client or participant has approved disclosure where required;
- baseline and result evidence are traceable;
- limitations are visible;
- no invented quote, statistic, or metric is included;
- burden transfer and unintended outcomes are considered;
- the claim is no larger than the evidence;
- and the post-contact Goodness Review has been completed.
